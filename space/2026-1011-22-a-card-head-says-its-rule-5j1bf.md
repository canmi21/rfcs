## Question

What does the head of a card on a design page say, and in what voice?

## Options

- A title alone.
- A title over a paragraph explaining the rule and how it was reached.
- A title, one sentence of the rule as it stands, and the RFC behind it.

## Evidence

Sentences that argued for their rule, or told how a value was derived, read as a case being made,
and a reader looking for the rule had to find it inside the reasons. A sentence counting its
specimens, or carrying several numbers, crowded the one number that meant something, an APCA Lc or a
ratio of ΔL\*. A first card named after its page said the name a third time, after the sidebar and
the tab. Two cards on a page opening on the same word read as one card twice.

## Decision

- **The head is the title, one sentence of the rule, and the RFC**, the RFC at its top right.
- **The sentence is the shortest that says the rule**, in commas and periods, with no derivation;
  stated as it stands, in the present, objectively, neither a command nor a reason; **plain**, as one
  would say it aloud; **with at most one number, and only one that means something**.
- **No two cards on a page open on the same word, and no card takes its page's name**, the first
  least of all.
