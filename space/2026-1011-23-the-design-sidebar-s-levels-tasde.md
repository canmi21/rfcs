## Question

The design pages sit under four layers and their categories. Which of them is a level the sidebar
steps into, what does a deeper level hold, and how does the reader tell what an entry leads to?

## Options

- **Levels**: a layer as a level, holding its categories; or a category as a level, a layer only
  naming the way back.
- **Home**: kept in every level; or only at the top.
- **Marking**: no mark on an entry that holds a list; or a chevron on it.
- **Pages not drawn yet**: left out until drawn; or named and not linked.
- **Group headings**: a link tall; or half a link tall.

## Evidence

With one of four layers drawn, a layer as a level left the top level all but empty. A level deeper
had no use for Home. Without a mark, a reader opened entries to learn which held a list. Leaving out
what is not drawn hid what a category will hold; named in the subtle tone and refusing the pointer,
it shows it before it holds it, as a layer with no page does in the top bar. A heading a link tall
broke the list's rhythm; half a link kept it in half steps.

## Decision

A category is a level; a layer takes a segment of the address and names the way back, drawing no
list. Home stands at the top level only. An entry that enters a level ends in a chevron. A page not
drawn yet is named and not linked, in the subtle tone, the pointer showing `not-allowed`. A group's
heading is half a link tall, its words at the bottom of that half.
